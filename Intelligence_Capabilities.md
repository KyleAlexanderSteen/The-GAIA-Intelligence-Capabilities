A capability is a context-dependent capacity to achieve a class of
outcomes. Capability is not equivalent to a rehearsed skill, permission,
or universal competence.

# 1. Scope and interpretation

Scope: Version 1.0 foundation ontology, dated 25 October 2026. This is
an authored synthesis with source-informed distinctions, not an
exhaustive inventory, official standard, diagnostic instrument, or
validated comparison of people and systems. No actor is asserted to
possess every listed property.

Terminology: Intelligence is the project term for engineered
computational intelligence systems, including models, agents, and
embodied systems. Human intelligence remains intelligence; Human and
Intelligence are operational actor classes here, not a claim that humans
lack intelligence. Source titles are retained in source records where
needed.

Type discipline: A node belongs to a concept scheme, not automatically
to an individual actor. Assignments to actors require separate evidence.
Branches and leaves use narrowerThan for classification. Cross-category
links are typed relations, not subclass assertions.

Evidence: Each actor assignment must record actor/version, task,
environment, inputs, tools, access, resources, evaluator, date, evidence
URI, uncertainty, limitations, and outcome. Unassessed is not zero and
is not evidence of inability.

Inclusion: Human variation, disability, assistive technology, culture,
training, and context matter. Intelligence performance depends on
version, configuration, scaffolding, tools, and deployment. Neither
fluent output nor shared labels establishes consciousness, feeling,
personhood, rights, or professional qualification.

# 2. Actor-specific interpretation

Capabilities describe measurable functions, not presumed subjective
faculties. Memory may be context storage or external retrieval; social
coordination means observable interaction behavior; self-monitoring
means performance checking, not demonstrated self-awareness. Physical
action requires an embodiment or external actuator.

# 4. Assignment and assessment record

Required fields: assignment_id; concept_id; actor_id; actor_class;
system_version_if_applicable; context; task; independent_or_assisted;
evidence_status; evidence_reference; observed_outcome; evaluation_date;
evaluator; limitations; review_date.

Evidence states: not assessed / claimed / observed once / replicated
within context / externally reviewed / contradicted / deprecated. These
are workflow labels, not a universal ordinal scale. Report success rate
and uncertainty only with a defined sample and protocol; do not
aggregate incompatible tasks into a single intelligence score.

# 5. Cross-file mapping and maintenance

Use the workbook Nodes sheet as the concept index and Edges sheet as the
typed relation register. Read contextualCounterpart as conceptual
comparison only. usesKnowledge, operationalizes, and mayBeEnabledBy link
the four schemes without conflating them. A missing edge means
unrecorded, not impossible.

Preserve stable IDs. Add narrower nodes rather than silently changing
meanings. Deprecate superseded concepts with replacement links; retain
provenance. Review duplication, culture-specific assumptions,
evidentiary gaps, permissions, and accessibility before release.

Optional GAIA facets: Divergence (explore alternatives), Insurgence
(propose challenges to assumptions, without implying unauthorized
action), Allegiance (record commitments and authority), Convergence
(integrate evidence), Ascendence (evaluate improvement). These are
project-authored analytical tags, not empirically validated stages or
rankings.
